The five curves, same $1,000, same year

Three of the five are NEAR, which is what "top five" honestly looks like on a one-year screen: the coin matters as much as the rule. NEAR fell 20.6 percent over the year and the 4h sleeves still made 68 to 87 percent on it, by sitting out the declines rather than riding them.

The rule has to work on the other 28 coins too

Breadth is the test that separates a rule from a fitted curve. Every one of the top-five cells is a rule that was not profitable on most coins: the NEAR pullback made money on 21 percent of them, and no 4h configuration cleared 56 percent. At the daily chart the same pullback and breakout logic flips: lab breakout 120h s3 t5 was positive on 83 percent of 29 coins (median +2.9 percent, average drawdown -5.7), and s2 t3 on 69 percent. Small per coin, but it is the only thing in 1,534 cells that looks like a market property rather than a coin property.

Trades matter here: those 1d breakout runs average 16.6 trades a year against 10 to 20 for the 4h cells, and only 8 of 29 were positive in both halves, so the edge is thin and it leans on the second-half rally. It is a family to build on, not a finished sleeve.

The strategy families from the two lists

Everything above is trend, breakout, pullback, momentum and mean reversion. The lists added seven more families; four are testable on long-only spot with the data we keep, and I ran them:

Not testable here, and worth saying why: funding-rate arbitrage and market making need perps, shorts or maker rebates the spot book does not have; liquidation cascades need open-interest and funding data we do not store; statistical arbitrage in its real form needs shorts for the other leg. A long-only "buy the laggard of the pair" variant is the only piece that fits, and it is a coin-pair study of its own.

Where this leaves the year. The most profitable cells are 4h NEAR sleeves (+68 to +87 percent, drawdowns of 15 to 30) and they are coin-specific: the rules lose on most other coins. The only broad edge is the 1d breakout family, 69 to 83 percent of coins positive with a +3 percent median, too small to pay a living alone but the right base for the next step.

What I would ship first: JUP capdip 240h d20 rr1.5 (+28.1 percent, 35 trades, 63 percent win, -9.0 drawdown, positive on all five charts and identical to its neighbour median) as the one sleeve with evidence at its back, then the 1d breakout as a separate small size until it earns more.

What not to ship: NEAR sma_cross 96/384h - +87 percent is ten trades, one of which is most of the return, and its first half was negative. That is a lottery ticket priced as a result.
