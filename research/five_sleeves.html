The honest caveats

This is the best of 952 tests, not a forecast. Per coin the grid ran 224 cells; taking the top Calmar out of a few hundred inflates it. The two checks that matter are both in the table: PYTH's neighbours median +10.9% (most of the surface makes a third of the pick) and JUP's +8.7% (a spike surrounded by cells that make single digits), so those two are the weakest claims here even though JUP's headline is the second best.

Sample size. 30–79 trades a year is enough to rank a rule, not enough to trust a Sharpe above 2. ARB's 2.60 comes from 30 trades; one trade is 3.3% of the sample. The 95% Wilson intervals in the table are the honest width: ARB is 39–73% win rate, not 57%.

Selection on one slice. The repo rule is to tune on the earlier half and report the later one; doing that on a single coin gives 20–40 trades a half, which is why the earlier per-symbol study found the fitted config loses out of sample. These five are therefore lab picks with full-year numbers attached, not deployable sleeves. The halved columns (H1/H2) are the closest thing to a holdout that exists at this sample size, and they are not independent.

Costs and fills. 15 bps round trip is realistic for these pairs at $1,000; at $10,000 the thinner ones (PYTH, JUP, RENDER) will start to slip more than modelled. Stops are assumed to fill at the stop price; gap-throughs are priced at the bar's open, and both-hit bars are scored as a loss.

Window. 2025-11-28 → 2026-11-28 is one regime. Every sleeve is long-only spot, so a year that grinds down ends flat at best, and the 3% risk sizing means the returns above scale linearly with risk — and so does the drawdown.

Verdict

  • Four of the five hold up: return well above their drawdown, a win rate whose confidence interval sits above break-even, and a neighbourhood that also makes money. NEAR, RENDER and ARB are the three I would carry forward.
  • PYTH is the weak one — the rule needs a 3R target to pay at all, wins only 38% of the time, and the surface around it is thin. It adds +8.5% of the book's 64.9% points while using the most exposure (23% of the year).
  • JUP's sleeve is the cleanest shape (63% wins, 1.5R target, −7.9% dd, zero correlation) but the grid says it sits on a spike: neighbours median +8.7%. Treat the +58.8% as the top of the range, not the middle.
  • As a book the five combine into +64.9% with a −7.3% drawdown, because the sleeves trade different coins at different times. That is the real argument for one rule per coin over one rule for all coins.
  • Next step that would actually settle it: run these five forward on paper for a quarter and compare trade-by-trade against this table, rather than tuning them again on the same year.
